The failure is the **discontinuity**, not any single screen: the redesign stops at the tab bar. A returning student's daily loop — open Quran, check a meeting, check progress — happens entirely outside the world the landing page promises.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Desktop has no page container: three screens stretch a phone layout to 1440
**What:** `quran.tsx:85`, `meetings.tsx:67`, `account.tsx:120` and `_layout.web.tsx:43` all use full-bleed `p-4` with no max width. Measured: Quran's CTA is a 1400px bar; the Account profile card is **1408px** wide (x=16→1424); Meetings day cells are ~195px wide; the navbar's first and last tabs sit **1244px** apart, home at x=0 and Account at x=1440. Home alone is centred in `max-w-6xl` with a 144px gutter. So the nav, the pages, and Home have three different grids.
**Why it matters:** A student on a laptop sees three stretched phones with 1400px-wide hit areas, and the app looks unfinished next to the landing page. Wide rows also break reading: a settings label cannot be scanned across 1400px.
**Fix:** One shared container. Home keeps `max-w-6xl`; Quran and Account get `mx-auto w-full max-w-3xl` on their content containers; Meetings becomes `max-w-5xl` with `md:flex-row` (calendar `md:w-[22rem]`, list `flex-1`); the navbar gets an inner `mx-auto w-full max-w-6xl` row so tabs align with page content.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P0] Account's dark-mode row labels are effectively invisible (1.23:1)
**What:** Measured on `/account` in dark at both 390 and 1440, and reproduced in an en-dark probe: `التقدّم` / `Progress` renders `#000000` on `#002204` = **1.23:1**; the supporting line `حفظك عبر الوقت` is `#6B7280` on `#002204` = **3.53:1**. The `@expo/ui` `List` web fallback is painting black labels while the app is dark-themed. Light mode is fine.
**Why it matters:** The two settings rows are unreadable, not merely low-contrast. This is the screen a teacher opens to reach progress, and in Arabic dark mode it is the default experience.
**Fix:** Stop relying on `@expo/ui` `List` for themed web rows: render Account rows with the app's own `Text`/`Icon` in `flex-row`/`flex-row-reverse` containers on web (native keeps the SwiftUI/Compose list), or pass an explicit foreground colour through `Host`. Then assert the pair in both schemes.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] The halqa world ends at the tab bar
**What:** The navbar is `bg-card` with `bg-muted` active pills; Quran is a bordered `Card` with a solid green button; Meetings is a stock grid; Account is a default list. Design specificity drops from 4/5 to 1–1.5/5 the moment you leave Home. The active tab on Home is a grey pill on white — the one state the user checks constantly is the least designed.
**Why it matters:** The redesign promises a kuttab; the next tap delivers a generic dashboard. That gap reads as "demo" and undermines trust in the whole product, especially for a teacher evaluating it.
**Fix:** Carry the material grammar, not the canopy: active tab gets a brass hairline underline plus `text-foreground` (no grey pill); section labels become brass overlines instead of grey caps; replace the Quran/Account bordered cards with hairline sections like the verse band; adopt one display step for page titles (Home's 60px vs the app's 24px is not a scale). Keep the reader and calendar surfaces plain.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish`

### [P1] The Quran tab is the emptiest screen in the product
**What:** `/quran` shows a title, "Continue reading", "Page 1", one full-width "Open the mushaf" button, and a caption. No surah, juz, page context, or preview. At 1440 that is a 1400px green slab in a white void; at 390 it is a third of a screen.
**Why it matters:** This is the screen the product exists for. "Page 1" is not a position a student can recognise, and there is nothing to make opening the mushaf feel like returning to a place rather than launching a tool.
**Fix:** Use data that already exists: name the last-read position ("Al-Fatihah · page 1" via the existing surah mapping), show progress within the juz, and let the existing page scan act as a small preview beside the CTA. Make the preview itself the open control. Keep the swipe hint as a caption.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] Home's lower half is unresolved at 768/1440
**What:** The station column is ~656px wide, so chevrons float ~600px from their labels; "Sign in" lands at x≈793 between the two columns at 768, unrelated to either heading; the verse band sits in a 384px column while the left column below the stations is blank, so the page ends in a void.
**Why it matters:** The landing page's second viewport is where a visitor decides to enter; right now it looks like content ran out. The stranded Sign in also makes the account path hard to find.
**Fix:** Cap the station list at `md:max-w-md`, keep "Sign in" in the header row but constrain that row to the same width, and align the verse band's top edge with the "Your circle" heading so the two columns share a baseline. Let the verse band take the remaining height rather than floating at the top.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

## Minor Observations

- Light-mode contrast: "Verse of the Day" is **2.49:1** (brass `#C79E4D` on white, 11px), "HALQA" **3.98:1**, "Hijri" **4.35:1**. Brass needs an ink variant for text on light grounds.
- Meetings' only visible state today is a grey tray glyph that reads as a broken image, with no next step ("No meetings on this day." full stop).
- Arabic calendar prints Western digits (`1, 2, 3`) inside "نوفمبر 2026"; `formatDayNumber` is not locale-aware.
- The segmented control is 32px tall — below the 44pt target the rest of the app claims.
- Dark mode is unfinished: `canopy` (`oklch .18`) and `background` (`oklch .2`) are too close, so the hero's rounded edge disappears; only light mode carries the parchment ground.
- The Latin "HALQA" overline stays Latin in Arabic UI.
- Arabic hero title is `كُتّاب عُثمان` while the app name elsewhere is "Othman"; the wordmark has two spellings in one product.
- Account has no page title; the first thing on screen is a card.
